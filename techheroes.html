Tech heroes / Public interest technology

Afua Bruce

Engineering with communities in mind.

Technology should serve people

Afua Bruce is an engineer, author, and public interest technologist whose work connects technology, policy, and society. She has held senior science and technology positions at DataKind, the White House, the FBI, and IBM. She earned a computer engineering degree from Purdue University and an MBA from the University of Michigan.

Bruce’s work challenges the idea that technical progress can be measured only by speed or profit. Her book, The Tech That Comes Next, explores how technologists, philanthropists, and changemakers can help build a more equitable world. Her public-facing work also encourages communities and organizations to use technology responsibly.

She is an ally advancing equity through technology: her approach connects technical decisions to the people and communities affected by them. Her experience in U.S. government and nonprofit settings makes that approach relevant to questions about who benefits from public systems and whose needs get overlooked.

What stands out to us is her focus on people. For someone interested in IT and cybersecurity, her work is a reminder that a successful system should help the people it is supposed to serve. We would apply that lesson by asking users about barriers before deciding what a solution should look like.
